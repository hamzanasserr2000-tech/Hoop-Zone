Basketball Rules

Learn the basic rules of basketball and understand the game better!

🏀 Dribbling

Learn how players move the ball around the court.

What is Dribbling?

Dribbling is the act of bouncing the basketball with one hand while moving around the court. It allows players to move with the ball and create scoring opportunities.

What is Double Dribble?

A double dribble violation happens when a player starts dribbling again after ending their dribble and gaining control of the ball, unless an exception under the rules applies.

What Happens After a Violation?

The opposing team is generally awarded possession of the ball after a double dribble violation.

Example: A player dribbles the ball, catches it with both hands, and then starts dribbling again. This is generally a double dribble.

⏱️ Shot Clock

Teams must attempt a shot before time runs out.

What Is the Shot Clock?

The shot clock is a timer that limits how long a team can control the ball before attempting a shot. In the NBA, teams generally have 24 seconds to attempt a shot that meets the shot-clock rules.

When Does the Clock Reset?

The shot clock may reset to 24 seconds after the opposing team gains control of the ball. In certain situations, it can reset to 14 seconds instead. The reset depends on what happens during the play.

What Happens When Time Runs Out?

If the team fails to attempt a qualifying shot before the shot clock expires, it generally loses possession of the ball.

Example: A team has 24 seconds to attempt a shot. If the clock reaches zero without a qualifying shot attempt, a shot-clock violation generally occurs.

🚫 Fouls

Understand illegal contact during the game.

What Is a Foul?

A foul happens when a player breaks certain rules involving illegal physical contact or unsportsmanlike behavior during a basketball game.

Types of Fouls

Personal Foul: Illegal physical contact with an opponent, such as pushing or holding.

Offensive Foul: An illegal contact committed by a player whose team has possession of the ball, such as an illegal charge.

Technical Foul: A penalty for certain unsportsmanlike conduct or rule violations that do not necessarily involve physical contact.

What Happens After a Foul?

The penalty depends on the type of foul and the situation. It may result in free throws, a change of possession, or other penalties.

Example: If a defender illegally pushes an opponent, the referee may call a personal foul.

🎯 Free Throws

Learn how players score points from the free-throw line.

What Is a Free Throw?

A free throw is an unopposed shot taken from the free-throw line after certain fouls or penalties. The player shoots without a defender trying to block the shot directly.

How Many Points Is It Worth?

Each successful free throw is worth one point. Players may receive one, two, or three free throws, depending on the type of foul and the situation.

Free Throw Rules

The shooter must stay behind the free-throw line until the ball touches the basket or the backboard. Players must also follow the timing and positioning rules during the attempt.

Example: If a player makes two free throws, their team scores two points.

📏 Out of Bounds

Find out what happens when the ball leaves the court.

What Is Out of Bounds?

The ball is out of bounds when it touches the floor or an object outside the boundary lines, or when a player holding the ball steps on or outside a boundary line.

What Happens When the Ball Goes Out?

Play stops, and the opposing team generally gets possession of the ball if a player was the last to touch it before it went out of bounds.

How Does Play Restart?

The team awarded possession usually throws the ball in from outside the court at the designated spot, following the referee's instructions.

Example: A player passes the ball, but it goes over the sideline without touching another player. The opposing team generally gets the ball.

🚶 Traveling

Learn about illegal steps while holding the ball.

What Is Traveling?

Traveling is a violation that occurs when a player holding the ball moves their feet illegally under the rules, such as taking too many steps without dribbling.

What Is the Pivot Foot?

A pivot foot is the foot a player must keep in contact with the floor, according to the applicable footwork rules, while turning or preparing to pass or shoot.

What Happens After Traveling?

The opposing team is generally awarded possession of the ball after a traveling violation.

Example: A player catches the ball, takes several steps without legally dribbling, and commits a traveling violation.

Basketball Positions

Discover the five positions and learn the role of each player!

01

Point Guard (PG)

The Playmaker

The point guard organizes the offense, controls the ball, and creates scoring opportunities for teammates.

Key Skills: Passing, Dribbling, Vision

Example: Stephen Curry

02

Shooting Guard (SG)

The Scorer

The shooting guard focuses on scoring, shooting from distance, and creating opportunities on offense.

Key Skills: Shooting, Scoring, Defense

Example: Kobe Bryant

03

Small Forward (SF)

The All-Rounder

The small forward is a versatile player who contributes to scoring, passing, rebounding, and defense.

Key Skills: Agility, Scoring, Defense

Example: LeBron James

04

Power Forward (PF)

The Strong Forward

The power forward helps with rebounding, interior scoring, and defending opponents near the basket.

Key Skills: Strength, Rebounding, Defense

Example: Tim Duncan

05

Center (C)

The Rim Protector

The center usually plays near the basket, protects the rim, grabs rebounds, and scores close to the hoop.

Key Skills: Blocking, Rebounding, Strength

Example: Shaquille O'Neal
